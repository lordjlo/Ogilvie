# Scenario Planning Brief: Ogilvie Fleet
**Date:** April 2026 | **Horizon:** 3 Years (to Q1 2029) | **Key Question:** Can Ogilvie Fleet defend and grow its independent position through the EV transition and sector consolidation, or does structural pressure compress margins and force a strategic exit?

---

## Executive Summary
[ASSESSED] Ogilvie Fleet enters its 3-year planning horizon from a position of genuine strength — record fleet size (25,000 vehicles including managed), £247m turnover, 98% customer retention, and a deliberate pivot toward a total mobility ecosystem (ReLease, Rental Select+, MiSalarySacrifice). The base case sees modest but resilient growth to ~28,000–30,000 vehicles by 2029, with margin compression from EV residual value losses partially offset by salary sacrifice and used EV lease income. The bull case requires EV residuals to stabilise faster than consensus and salary sacrifice SME penetration to accelerate sharply — delivering fleet size above 35,000 and turnover approaching £320m. The bear case is driven by a specific, non-generic risk: a second wave of EV residual value deterioration (used EV supply projected to increase 178% by 2027) colliding with a funding cost squeeze, forcing balance sheet stress on an independently capitalised business that cannot absorb losses the way a bank-backed lessor can. Leadership's most urgent task today is hedging the residual value book and accelerating fee-based revenue that does not carry asset risk.

### Bear Case: "Balance Sheet Squeeze" — ~25% Probability
[DATA / ASSESSED]

**Narrative:**
The bear case is structurally specific, not generic. It is driven by the collision of three well-evidenced risks:

First, used EV supply increases by the forecast 178% between 2025 and 2027 — a function of the salary sacrifice and fleet leasing wave that began in 2022–2024 hitting its 3-year de-fleet cycle simultaneously. Demand for used EVs fails to keep pace. EV residual values fall a further 28% from already-depressed levels, as Fleet News and industry analysts explicitly warned. Ogilvie's EV book (219% YoY growth trajectory) means a disproportionate share of its funded assets are affected. Unlike Lex Autolease (Lloyds Banking Group-backed) or Arval (BNP Paribas-backed), Ogilvie does not have a bank parent to absorb residual value losses or provide emergency liquidity. Every £10m of residual value shortfall vs provision hits net assets directly.

Second, BIK tax rates rise faster than the published schedule post-2027, as the Government under fiscal pressure revisits the salary sacrifice benefit. This collapses the economics of Ogilvie's fastest-growing product line at the moment of maximum exposure, leaving a large salary sacrifice book written at rates predicated on 3–5% BIK facing dramatically lower take-up for renewals.

Third, a private equity-backed competitor (or a bank-backed lessor expanding downstream) enters the SME segment with technology-driven pricing and a captive funding advantage. Ogilvie's customer retention falls from 98% to 88–90% — which sounds small but represents ~2,200 vehicles walking and £22m+ of contracted revenue at risk per renewal cycle.

The result: turnover growth stalls, EV de-fleet losses erode net assets from £76m toward £50–55m, the company becomes a more attractive but also more pressured acquisition target. The family ownership structure becomes a governance constraint — capital needed for reinvestment competes with historic dividend expectations.

**Risk Triggers:**
- Manheim/Cox Automotive used EV auction prices fall below 30% of original list price for 2–3 year old BEVs on two consecutive quarterly auctions (early warning: Q3 or Q4 2026)
- HM Treasury announces review of BIK salary sacrifice EV treatment in an Autumn Budget statement

## Decision Framework

**If Bull signals dominate (used EV values >38%, SS growth >25% QoQ, BIK frozen, base rate cutting):**
Deploy the £49.4m cash reserve aggressively. Target one acquisition of a regional independent lessor with 3,000–5,000 vehicles and a complementary SME client base. Accelerate MiSalarySacrifice as a standalone national product with marketing investment. Set FN50 top 10 as a KPI for 2028.

**If Base case holds (EV values stabilising, SS growing at 10–15%, rates easing gradually):**
Execute the current strategic plan with discipline. Prioritise fee-income products (fleet management, consulting, driver services) that improve revenue quality without adding balance sheet risk. Hold cash reserves as dry powder for opportunistic acquisition. Continue the ReLease and Rental Select+ build-out — these products are structurally correct regardless of scenario.

**If Bear signals emerge (EV auction prices <32%, BIK review announced, competitor entering SME, retention slipping):**
Immediately de-risk the forward book: tighten EV residual value assumptions on new contract pricing (raise rates), reduce EV concentration in new fleet additions, and shift more disposal risk to lessees via maintained lease products or shorter 24-month terms. Evaluate an equity or debt raise to protect the balance sheet ahead of the EV de-fleet cycle peak (2027). Consider proactive conversations with a strategic acquirer on Ogilvie's terms rather than under duress.

**Hedge strategy — actions that work across all three scenarios:**
1. **Price EV residual risk into all new contracts from today.** Every contract written with optimistic EV residuals is a bear case accelerant. Use CAP HPI Black Book downside ranges, not mid-point, for contracts written in 2026–2027.
2. **Build fee-based revenue to 25%+ of gross margin by 2028.** Fleet management, driver services, EV consultancy, telematics — income streams with no asset risk. These pay off in the base and bull cases and act as a buffer in the bear case.
3. **Do not over-concentrate the salary sacrifice book beyond 20% of total on-risk fleet without a BIK rate hedge (contractual pass-through clauses if BIK changes >1% ahead of schedule).**
4. **Formalise the governance and succession structure.** The Ogilvie Group family structure is an asset for culture but a liability if capital decisions are constrained by dividend requirements during a bear case stress. A clearly documented capital allocation policy removes ambiguity.

## Key Drivers — Detailed Assessment

### 1. EV Residual Value Risk [DATA]
- Used EV values fell 60% between Sep 2022 and Sep 2024. Industry profits fell 32% (£645m) in the most recent FN50 period, overwhelmingly driven by EV RV losses.
- UK EV average depreciation: 38–42% over 3 years, vs 35–40% for petrol — but with far higher variance and downside tail.
- The structural threat: used EV supply is forecast to increase 178% over 2025–2027 as the 2022–2024 fleet EV wave hits de-fleet. Demand is "struggling to keep pace."
- For Ogilvie specifically: with 219% YoY EV fleet growth, the company's book is now disproportionately EV-weighted relative to its historical profile. This is the single largest P&L risk variable.
- Partial mitigation: ReLease (re-leasing returned EVs rather than selling into weak auction market) is a structurally intelligent response that converts a disposal problem into a revenue stream.

### 2. Sector Consolidation [ASSESSED]
- ALD acquisition of LeasePlan (2023) created a 300,000+ vehicle entity. Top 10 lessors hold 64% of UK market.
- Broker consolidation accelerating: GVG acquired Fleet Alliance (Jun 2025); Rivervale and others signalling further deals.
- Ogilvie's FN50 rank of ~14th is defensible but not growing fast enough to reach the scale threshold (50,000+ vehicles) that would make it a tier-1 consolidator rather than a potential consolidation target.
- The independent segment's structural advantage — service quality, flexibility, SME relationships — is real but not permanent. Bank-backed lessors are investing in SME propositions.

### 3. Interest Rates / Cost of Funding [DATA]
- Base rate: 3.75% (Mar 2026), held consensus through end 2026, expected drift to 3.0–3.25% by 2028.
- Every 50bps reduction in the base rate improves Ogilvie's funding spread and narrows the gap vs captive/bank-backed competitors who access cheaper institutional funding.
- Unlike Lex Autolease (Lloyds) or Arval (BNP Paribas), Ogilvie funds through commercial facilities. This means its funding cost is structurally ~50–100bps higher than bank-backed peers — a persistent disadvantage in a commodity lease pricing environment.

### 4. Customer Fleet Electrification Pace [DATA]
- Fleet corporate cars: 75%+ new orders are BEV in 2025. ZEV mandate 33% target for 2026 (all new cars, not just fleet).
- SME adoption lagging — Ogilvie's core market. The SME electrification wave is coming but is 12–24 months behind large corporates.
- BIK rates: EV at 3% in 2025/26 rising to 9% by 2029/30. This trajectory means the financial case for EVs weakens over time, particularly relative to the 2021–2023 window when 1–2% BIK made the calculus overwhelming.
- Implication for Ogilvie: the SME EV adoption wave arriving in 2026–2028 is Ogilvie's growth opportunity — but it needs to be captured at the right RV assumptions or it becomes a bear case accelerant.

### 5. Competitor Moves [ASSESSED]
- The "super broker" model (GVG, Rivervale) is growing its share of volume through the lessor channel (from 13.7% in 2024 to 15.2% in 2025). These are not direct competitors yet — they route business to lessors like Ogilvie — but as they vertically integrate, they could become direct.
- Volkswagen Financial Services (200,000+ UK fleet vehicles) and Stellantis Financial Services are expanding downstream. Captive finance for EV brands (Tesla Direct, BYD Finance emerging) could disrupt traditional lessor pricing in specific EV segments.
- No confirmed competitor has targeted Ogilvie's specific geography/segment niche. This is a 2027–2028 risk, not immediate.
