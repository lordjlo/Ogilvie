## TOP 5 "SO WHAT" FINDINGS

### 1. The EV residual value risk is the single largest financial threat
Used EV supply is forecast to increase 178% by 2027. EV values already fell 60% between Sep 2022 and Sep 2024. The entire UK FN50 saw profits fall 91% in 2025 on residual value losses. Ogilvie's £76M net asset base and £49M cash position is defensible under the base case — but under the bear case (a second RV deterioration wave), losses of £18–28M above provision would materially compress net assets. Unlike Lex Autolease, Arval, or Ayvens, Ogilvie has no bank parent to absorb the excess. The book must be stress-tested now.

**Therefore:** Re-price EV residual value assumptions on the forward book before the 2027 used-EV supply surge, and commission an independent bear-case stress test this quarter.

---

### 2. Ogilvie is a compelling, near-term acquisition target — and may not control the timing
UK fleet leasing has undergone structural consolidation: Ayvens (ALD + LeasePlan, £4.1bn), Lloyds Transport (Tusker, £300M), GVG (Fleet Alliance, June 2025). The remaining independents of scale are Zenith (Bridgepoint, PE exit overdue) and Ogilvie. At £247M revenue, 25,000 vehicles, and 98% retention, Ogilvie occupies a "too big to ignore, too small to compete at the top" position that historically precedes a sale. Novuna Vehicle Solutions (Hitachi/MUFG-backed, fastest-growing FN50) is identified as the highest-probability acquirer — they lack Scottish presence and SME depth that Ogilvie owns outright. Valuation: £200–£320M, benchmarked against the Tusker comparable (£300M for 23,000 vehicles). Family ownership is the structural barrier — and the eventual catalyst.

**Therefore:** The CEO's most consequential decision in the next 12 months is whether to proactively shape a sale or partnership conversation, or explicitly commit to independence with a funded capital plan to resist consolidation pressure — because the current posture (neither) leaves Ogilvie reactive to acquirers who will time the approach to their advantage.

---

### 3. Real power sits above the MD title — the July 2025 restructure was not a leadership handover
Nick Hardy became MD in July 2025, but the power architecture did not change. Duncan Ogilvie (sole beneficial shareholder) and Gordon Stephen (elevated to Chairman, retaining all board seats) remain the decision-making core. John Watson (34-year Finance Director) is the operational axis — and has no visible succession plan. Andy Stephen holds the AI/technology mandate. Any senior commercial engagement that targets only the MD title will not reach the people who decide.

**Therefore:** Stakeholder mapping confirms the decision power sits with Ogilvie/Stephen/Watson — the three-person inner circle must be the engagement focus for any strategic conversation.

---

### 4. Simultaneous leadership transition + technology build = highest execution risk window
Ogilvie is executing on five fronts simultaneously: ONWRD acquisition integration (Dec 2025), a new MD bedding in, salary sacrifice team doubling, south-of-England expansion (6 hires), and a board-mandated AI/technology programme (Andy Stephen's remit) with no visible talent pipeline behind it. The Technology & Systems Director role was only created in July 2025. Culture is genuinely strong (six consecutive "Outstanding" Best Companies accreditations, 719/1,000 engagement). But execution bandwidth is stretched, and the absence of any software engineers, data analysts, or product managers in public hiring suggests the AI mandate is aspirational, not resourced.

**Therefore:** The AI/technology capability gap is the most strategically significant internal risk — not culture, not retention — because it is the one area where a well-resourced competitor or acquirer can permanently outpace Ogilvie before the company builds its own capability.

---

### 5. Fleet Alliance's acquisition creates a Scottish media vacuum Ogilvie should own
Fleet Alliance — Ogilvie's only Scottish peer in fleet leasing — was acquired by Global Vehicle Group in June 2025. This removes the one competitor that shared Ogilvie's "Scottish fleet" media identity. Trade press, Scottish business media, and industry bodies now have a natural gap. Ogilvie's media presence is consistently positive but thin — predominantly trade-facing, with near-zero mainstream business visibility. This is a strategic communications opportunity, not just a PR one: increased share-of-voice directly supports the acquisition narrative ("Scotland's defining fleet business") and recruitment positioning.

**Therefore:** A structured media strategy targeting Scottish business media, national fleet trade press, and salary sacrifice specialist audiences should be activated in Q2 2026 to capture the Fleet Alliance vacuum before a competitor fills it.
