## 1. Governance Structure `DATA`

Ogilvie Fleet Limited is a **wholly-owned subsidiary of Ogilvie Group Limited** (SC029219), which is in turn **100% owned by the Ogilvie family**. The beneficial owner of record is **Duncan Henderson Ogilvie**, grandson of the group's founder (est. 1953, Stirling, Scotland). There is no private equity, institutional shareholder, or external investor at any level of the group.

This is a classic **third-generation family business** operating as a private limited company. There is no publicly traded element. Financial reporting is filed at Companies House but the company does not publish detailed investor-facing accounts.

Key structural facts:
- Ogilvie Fleet is the primary revenue driver within the Ogilvie Group automotive division
- Fleet-adjacent subsidiaries include **First Vehicle Leasing** (PCH/personal contract hire), **Active Claims Solutions** (motor claims handling), and the newly acquired **ONWRD** (flexible rental/mobility, acquired December 2025)
- The Ogilvie Group umbrella also contains Ogilvie Construction, Ogilvie Homes, Net Defence, and Malcolm Hughes Land Surveys
- Group turnover: approximately £250m+; Ogilvie Fleet estimated turnover: ~£247m (Pomanda/Companies House filings)
- Approximately 25,000 vehicles on fleet (including managed vehicles); ~450 group employees

## 6. Succession Signals `DATA`

The July 2025 leadership restructure was the most significant succession event in Ogilvie Fleet's history. Key signals:

1. **Gordon Stephen stepped back** — After 26 years as MD and 32 years total, he transitioned to a Chairman role. He did not leave the business. His continued seats on the Ogilvie Group board and three subsidiary boards signal a controlled transition with continued oversight rather than a clean exit.

2. **Nick Hardy is the organic choice** — A 40-year fleet career insider, at Ogilvie since 2007. The appointment is a continuity play, not a transformation hire. No external candidate was considered publicly.

3. **The Stephen family reconfigured rather than departed** — Jill Stephen moved from Customer Services Director to Operations Director (now MD's number two). Andy Stephen became Technology & Systems Director with an explicit AI mandate. Both retain board seats. The family presence in the executive team is unchanged in weight but repositioned in function.

4. **Ashley Crookes' elevation is the only new blood** — Crookes was promoted from Director of Sales (North) into the S&M Director seat vacated by Hardy. He brings ~30 years of fleet experience but is not a disruptive appointment.

5. **ONWRD acquisition (December 2025)** — The acquisition of the Cambridgeshire-based mobility specialist signals M&A appetite and a pivot towards "total mobility ecosystem" positioning. Ian Bennett's rental director role is likely to expand in scope as ONWRD is integrated.

6. **No CFO hire identified** — Watson remains the sole finance voice at both group and fleet level. At 34+ years of tenure, succession risk in the finance function is significant but unaddressed. `ASSESSED`

## 9. Therefore Statement

**Therefore:** Ogilvie Fleet is governed by a tightly controlled family-and-loyalist power structure where meaningful strategic decisions flow through Duncan Ogilvie (sole owner), Gordon Stephen (now Chairman with continued group board seats), and John Watson (34-year finance anchor) — with Nick Hardy as the new operational figurehead who was selected precisely because he will not disrupt the culture; any approach to Ogilvie Fleet at CEO or board level must account for the fact that the real decision-makers are the founding family and two long-tenure insiders, not the Managing Director alone.
