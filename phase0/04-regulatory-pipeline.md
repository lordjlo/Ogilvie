## Executive Summary

`[DATA]` Ogilvie Fleet faces a compressing regulatory stack across three distinct domains simultaneously: (1) FCA motor finance obligations tightening around commission disclosure and consumer duty enforcement from 31 May 2026; (2) ZEV mandate escalation requiring 33% EV car registrations in 2026 and 38% in 2027, creating acute residual value and fleet composition pressure on leasing funders; and (3) HMRC tax architecture changes — VED reform, BiK rate escalation, and the emerging eVED mileage tax from April 2028 — reshaping the total cost of fleet ownership calculations that underpin every leasing contract Ogilvie writes. The most dangerous near-term exposure is not any single regulation in isolation, but the interaction between rising EV BiK rates, unstable used EV residual values, and ZEV mandate-driven supply distortions — all hitting simultaneously while customer appetite for full EV transition remains uneven. Leadership must act within 90 days to structurally address residual value exposure and ensure FCA Consumer Duty board-level evidence is in place before Q3 2026 supervisory focus sharpens.

## High-Impact Items — Detailed Analysis

### 1. FCA PS26/3 — Motor Finance Consumer Redress Scheme
`[DATA]`

**What it does:** Establishes two industry-wide redress schemes covering motor finance agreements between April 2007 and November 2024 where undisclosed discretionary commission arrangements (DCAs) caused consumer harm. Scheme 2 (April 2014 – November 2024) and Scheme 1 (April 2007 – March 2014). Estimated redress: £7.5–18bn industry-wide. Average consumer payment: ~£829.

**Current stage:** Final rules published 30 March 2026 (PS26/3). The pause on complaint handling lifted 31 May 2026.

**Probability:** Confirmed / in implementation.

**Critical distinction for Ogilvie Fleet:** Leasing agreements (contract hire / PCH / finance lease) are explicitly **excluded** from the redress scheme. The FCA directed firms to begin sending final responses to leasing complaints from 5 December 2025 under normal complaint handling rules. However, the reputational and operational spillover is real: customer confusion between "car finance" and "leasing" will generate complaints Ogilvie must handle at pace, and lender counterparties (funders) may face capital and liquidity strain from the wider scheme, affecting credit lines.

**Timeline:**
- 5 Dec 2025: Leasing complaint responses must be live
- 31 May 2026: General DCA complaint pause lifted
- 30 Jun 2026: Scheme 2 implementation period ends
- 30 Sep 2026: Lender redress calculations due to consumers (Scheme 2)
- 31 Oct 2026: Consumer acceptance window closes (Scheme 2)
- Nov 2026: Payments due (Scheme 2)
- Jan 2027: Payments due (Scheme 1 final resolution)

**Competitive effect:** Independent fleet funders with clean commission structures and transparent pricing are better positioned than lenders tied up in the retail DCA scheme. Ogilvie's position as an independent (no manufacturer or bank ownership) is a credibility differentiator — but only if actively communicated.

**Precedent risk:** FCA has signalled willingness to conduct market-wide redress exercises. Consumer Duty will be the vehicle for future interventions; failure to document good outcomes now creates future liability.

### 2. ZEV Mandate — Escalating Annual Targets (VETS Order 2023 as amended)
`[DATA]`

**What it does:** The Vehicle Emissions Trading Schemes (VETS) Order 2023 implements the ZEV mandate, requiring manufacturers to sell a minimum percentage of zero-emission vehicles each calendar year. Fines: £15,000 per car shortfall, £18,000 per van shortfall. Flexibility mechanisms include borrowing (up to 20% in 2027), banking, and credit trading between manufacturers.

**Targets:**
| Year | Cars (ZEV %) | Vans (ZEV %) |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| 2025 | 28% | 16% |
| 2026 | 33% | 24% |
| 2027 | 38% | 34% |
| 2028 | 52% | 46% |
| 2030 | 80% | 70% |

**Current stage:** In force and escalating. UK car and van markets met the 2025 (first-year) targets. 2026 compliance is active. The petrol/diesel new car ban remains confirmed for 2030.

**Probability:** Confirmed. Some SMMT/BVRLA lobbying for a review, but the 2030 ban and 2026-2027 targets are legislated.

**Impact on Ogilvie Fleet as a leasing company (funder obligations vs customer obligations):**

- **Funder / leasing company obligations:** Ogilvie Fleet does NOT hold a ZEV mandate obligation directly — the mandate applies to **vehicle manufacturers and importers** who register vehicles. However, as a volume fleet customer, Ogilvie's procurement decisions directly determine which manufacturer registrations count toward their ZEV compliance. Manufacturers will offer preferential pricing, faster delivery, and better terms on EVs to fleet buyers to hit their targets. ICE supply will be constrained and priced harder. This creates **indirect commercial leverage** for Ogilvie as a fleet buyer.

- **Residual value exposure:** The mandate forces EV supply faster than organic demand absorbs it. This suppresses used EV residual values — a direct cost to Ogilvie at end-of-lease disposition. BVRLA data confirms members are absorbing heavy losses on 2022-vintage EV remarket cycles. As Ogilvie writes longer-term leases (typically 36-48 months), contracts written in 2024-2025 at optimistic RV assumptions face mark-to-market losses in 2026-2028.

- **Customer (corporate lessee) obligations:** Corporate customers have no direct ZEV mandate obligation (unless they are fleet operators subject to other transport regulations). However, their SECR/ESG reporting requirements, corporate net-zero commitments, and salary sacrifice scheme uptake create demand signals that Ogilvie must model to price contracts correctly.

**Competitive effect:** Manufacturers prioritise fleet channels to move EV volume. Ogilvie's independence allows it to shop across all OEMs for best terms, unlike manufacturer-captive leasing arms. Risk: Ogilvie may be disadvantaged in ICE allocation as manufacturers deprioritise non-ZEV volume in fleet.

**Precedent risk:** Targets in 2028 (52% cars) represent a step-change. Any under-performance in 2027 by manufacturers triggers borrowing mechanisms that push compliance pressure into 2028-2029. If the 2030 petrol/diesel ban holds, the used EV residual value overhang will peak in 2028-2030 — precisely when large tranches of 2025-2027 fleet leases terminate.

### 3. HMRC Benefit-in-Kind Rate Escalation — Electric Vehicles
`[DATA]`

**What it does:** Sets the percentage of list price on which company car drivers pay Income Tax and employers pay Class 1A National Insurance Contributions. Zero-emission vehicles enjoy a preferential rate that is escalating annually from a historic low of 1% (2021/22).

**Rate schedule:**
| Tax Year | ZEV BiK Rate |
|----------|-------------|
| 2025/26 | 3% |
| 2026/27 | 4% |
| 2027/28 | 5% |
| 2028/29 | 7% |
| 2029/30 | 9% |

**Current stage:** 4% rate in force from 6 April 2026. All rates through 2029/30 are legislated.

**Impact on Ogilvie Fleet:** BiK rates do not directly determine lease rental pricing but materially affect customer demand signals. A £40,000 EV at 4% BiK generates a £1,600 taxable benefit — costing a 40% taxpayer £640/year (still highly attractive vs petrol equivalent at 28-37%). However:

- Salary sacrifice economics remain strong but erode marginally each year. Ogilvie's salary sacrifice fleet offering (a high-growth segment — industry salary sacrifice doubled in 2025) remains viable through 2027/28 but pricing guides must be updated each April.
- PHEVs with 70+ mile electric range remain in lower BiK bands (5-8% from 2026). Potential "PHEV resurgence" in fleet decisions creates a routing problem: PHEVs face elimination from new registrations in 2035 and have disputed real-world efficiency — but BiK rates may drive short-term demand.
- From April 2028, the proposed eVED (Electric Vehicle Excise Duty) of 3p/mile will layer onto BiK costs — the first time EVs face a running cost that scales with use. This is a structural pricing risk for long-mileage fleet leases.

### 4. eVED — Electric Vehicle Excise Duty (April 2028)
`[ASSESSED]`

**What it does:** A new mileage-based tax proposed at 3p/mile for fully electric company cars and 1.5p/mile for PHEVs, charged alongside existing VED. Budget 2025 announcement. Consultation closed 18 March 2026.

**Current stage:** Consultation complete; legislation not yet introduced as of April 2026. BVRLA has described it as "fleet-hostile." Final design is still subject to parliamentary process.

**Probability:** High (>75%) that some form of eVED launches April 2028. The government needs revenue as VED receipts fall with EV adoption and fuel duty erodes. The specific rate may change but the direction of travel is confirmed.

**Impact on Ogilvie Fleet:** Leases written today for 3-4 year terms through 2028-2030 face eVED cost exposure in the final 12-24 months of the contract. A 20,000 mile/year EV fleet driver would face an additional £600/year in eVED at 3p/mile — a cost that could sit with either the funder or the customer depending on how lease agreements are structured. Fleet contracts must include eVED contingency clauses now.

**Competitive effect:** Leasing companies that fail to build eVED into contract terms will absorb the cost themselves at end-of-lease or face contract disputes. Those with clear force majeure / regulatory change clauses are protected.

### 5. FCA Consumer Duty — Enforcement Phase 2026
`[DATA]`

**What it does:** The Consumer Duty (PS22/9) requires FCA-authorised firms to deliver good customer outcomes across four pillars: products and services, price and value, consumer understanding, and consumer support. The duty applies to all regulated activities including consumer credit, credit broking, and hire agreements with consumers.

**Scope for Ogilvie Fleet:** Ogilvie Fleet is FCA-authorised (consumer credit and credit broking). Fleet finance products (contract hire, finance lease, contract purchase offered to individual/SME consumers) are in scope. The Duty does not apply to pure B2B (large corporate) transactions — but the boundary is under active FCA consultation (H1 2026 paper on B2B / wholesale exclusions).

**2026 FCA priorities for Consumer Finance (from FCA Regulatory Priorities Report March 2026):**
- Demonstrating actual good customer outcomes (not just policies)
- Price and value assessments — are fleet finance products priced fairly relative to value delivered?
- Consumer understanding of total cost of ownership in EV vs ICE leases
- Complaint handling quality — especially as motor finance confusion generates indirect leasing complaints
- Board-level evidence of Consumer Duty oversight (FCA will request MI and board reports in supervisory visits)

**Timeline:**
- H1 2026: FCA consultation on B2B exclusions and co-manufacturer responsibilities in distribution chains
- Q3 2026: FCA thematic review of consumer finance sector outcomes expected
- Q4 2026: Final rules on Consumer Duty scope clarification
- 2027: FCA enforcement action against firms without demonstrable evidence of good outcomes

## Prepare Now — Compliance Actions

### Immediate (next 90 days — by end June 2026)

1. **Establish a Consumer Duty board-level MI pack** — The FCA is in active enforcement phase for Consumer Finance from Q3 2026. Ogilvie must be able to demonstrate at board level: (a) that fleet finance products deliver fair value; (b) that consumer-facing communications (salary sacrifice guides, quote tools) meet Consumer Understanding standards; (c) that complaint trends are tracked and acted upon. FCA supervisory visits will request this evidence. Without it, Ogilvie risks being in the first wave of enforcement examples.

2. **Audit leasing complaint handling process for FCA compliance** — With the DCA complaint pause lifting 31 May 2026, customer confusion between motor finance and leasing will spike. Ogilvie must have a triaging process that (a) correctly classifies leasing vs credit complaints; (b) issues final responses to leasing complaints within 8 weeks; (c) documents the outcome trail. A complaint handling failure now is a Consumer Duty failure.

3. **Update all salary sacrifice pricing guides and customer literature for April 2026 BiK change** — BiK has moved from 3% to 4%. All TCO calculators, customer quote tools, marketing materials, and scheme documentation must reflect 2026/27 rates. Include a forward schedule through 2029/30 so customers can model multi-year cost. Failure to communicate the escalation path is a Consumer Understanding risk under the Duty.

4. **Insert eVED contingency clauses into all new fleet lease agreements** — eVED (3p/mile for EVs) is not yet law but the consultation closed March 2026 and legislation is expected. Any 36-48 month lease written today will span April 2028. Without a regulatory change clause, Ogilvie absorbs the cost. Legal must draft and operations must implement this clause immediately.

5. **Commission a residual value stress test on current EV portfolio** — With ZEV mandate pushing EV supply above demand, used EV values are structurally suppressed. Ogilvie should model its current EV lease book against three RV scenarios: (a) current market; (b) 15% further decline; (c) 25% decline. Identify the contracts and vintages with worst exposure. This is both a balance sheet management action and a prerequisite for any future pricing model redesign.

### Near-term (3–12 months)

6. **Develop a ZEV mandate procurement strategy for 2027** — 2027 targets (38% cars / 34% vans) are materially harder. Manufacturers will offer preferential pricing and supply terms on EVs to fleet buyers who commit volumes early. Ogilvie's procurement team should be in structured conversations with OEMs by Q3 2026 to lock in 2027 pricing and allocation. ICE supply will tighten; customers wanting ICE alternatives face longer lead times and higher costs.

7. **Build Scope 3 fleet emissions reporting capability** — Ogilvie's large corporate clients face UK SRS obligations from January 2027 and will require fleet emissions data. Ogilvie should have a self-service or account-managed fleet emissions dashboard capability ready by Q4 2026. This is both a retention tool and a new commercial service. Data inputs required: vehicle type, fuel, CO2/km per vehicle, total fleet annual mileage, EV percentage.

8. **Review and update HMRC AER (Advisory Electricity Rate) reimbursement guidance to customers** — HMRC now distinguishes between home charging (7p/mile) and public charging (15p/mile) from March 2026. Fleet drivers and customers are frequently confused and under-reimbursing. Ogilvie's fleet management guidance, driver handbooks, and expense claim templates must be updated. A failure to guide customers here creates grey fleet tax risk for both Ogilvie and its clients.

9. **Audit marketing for FCA Anti-Greenwashing Rule compliance** — Any claim Ogilvie makes about "green fleet," "zero emissions," "carbon-neutral leasing," or similar must be consistent with actual product sustainability characteristics and substantiated. The FCA's anti-greenwashing rule (in force since May 2024) applies to all FCA-authorised firms. A review of website copy, sales collateral, and customer communications for greenwashing risk should be completed.

10. **Map all funder counterparty motor finance redress exposure** — Ogilvie does not offer HP or PCP (the products caught by the DCA redress scheme) but sources vehicles via relationships with funders who may be caught. Model the scenario where a key funder faces a significant capital call from the redress scheme: what is the credit facility impact? What are the alternative funder relationships? Document the contingency plan.

Ogilvie's account managers should be proactively advising eligible corporate clients to submit applications by Q4 2026 at the latest. This is a customer value-add touchpoint that also supports EV fleet adoption.

12. **Prepare Employment Rights Act 2025 employment contract review** — Key provisions will commence through 2026-2027. Ogilvie's HR and legal functions should have conducted a full contract and policy audit by Q4 2026. ## Wildcard Scenarios

### Wildcard 1 — ZEV Mandate Emergency Review / Relaxation
**Trigger:** If H1 2026 registration data shows the UK car market tracking materially below 33% ZEV share, combined with SMMT lobbying and political pressure from automotive trade unions, the government could announce an emergency review of 2026-2028 targets in Q3 2026.
**Impact on Ogilvie:** A mandate relaxation would increase ICE supply, stabilise used ICE values, and could cause used EV values to fall further as the ZEV floor is removed from manufacturer incentives. Would require immediate repricing of residual values across the entire fleet book.
**Probability:** ASSESSED at 30% for 2026; 40% for 2027 if market conditions deteriorate.

### Wildcard 2 — Supreme Court / High Court Motor Finance Appeal Reversal
**Trigger:** Although the Supreme Court issued its judgment in August 2025, residual litigation risk remains. A further legal challenge or judicial review of PS26/3 (the redress scheme itself) could delay or modify the scheme significantly.
**Impact on Ogilvie:** Leasing is excluded from the scheme, but a delay or collapse of funder redress payments would create financial strain at counterparty funders, potentially tightening credit facilities. Ogilvie should stress-test funder relationships.
**Probability:** ASSESSED at 20% for material scheme modification.

### Wildcard 3 — Sudden Used EV Market Correction (Cliff Event)
**Trigger:** A major battery safety recall, high-profile battery degradation class action, or OEM financial distress (e.g., a major EV manufacturer ceasing operations in the UK) could trigger a sudden 30-40% decline in used EV values within a 3-6 month period.
**Impact on Ogilvie:** Catastrophic exposure on any lease contracts priced with optimistic RV assumptions and without adequate provisions. Could crystallise balance sheet losses across the fleet book in a single reporting period.
**Probability:** LOW (15%) for a cliff event; HIGH (>70%) for continued gradual RV erosion over 24 months.

### Wildcard 4 — Mandatory Corporate Fleet Electrification Legislation
**Trigger:** Following the French model (RE2030 Act requiring 50% ZEV in fleets of 100+ cars by 2030), the UK government could introduce mandatory corporate fleet electrification targets, potentially from 2028-2030. The Transport Decarbonisation Plan already signals this direction.
**Impact on Ogilvie:** A mandatory corporate fleet EV percentage would be a demand acceleration event — beneficial for Ogilvie's leasing volumes but compressing lead times, supply availability, and potentially creating charging infrastructure constraints for customers.
**Probability:** ASSESSED at 35% for legislation by 2029 — low for 2026-2027.

### Wildcard 5 — NIC Employer Rate Change Affecting Salary Sacrifice Economics
**Trigger:** The employer NIC rate was raised to 15% in the October 2024 Budget effective April 2025. A further NIC rate change (up or down) in a future Budget would directly alter the employer NIC saving calculation in salary sacrifice schemes — the primary driver of employer adoption.
**Impact on Ogilvie:** A further increase would make salary sacrifice even more attractive (employers save more NIC). A reduction would slightly reduce the incentive. Any change requires immediate update to scheme calculators and customer guides.
**Probability:** ASSESSED at 25% for a further NIC change within 24 months.
